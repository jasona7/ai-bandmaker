Backstory

In the grimy backstreets of 1978 London, where the air was thick with revolution and cigarette smoke, emerged a sonic enigma known as The Velvet Echoes. Born in the fallout of punk's primal scream, they crafted a hypnotic neo-vintage tapestry that fused psychedelic rock's swirling kaleidoscope with the futuristic shimmer of synthwave. Fronting this aural expedition was Adrian "Whisper" Wyles, a charismatic enigma whose voice could melt the ice in any heart. Armed with his trusty Rickenbacker 360, he painted soundscapes that were as enchanting as they were haunting. His lyrics, a blend of surreal poetry and streetwise aphorisms, hinted at a world teetering on the brink of chaos and ecstasy. On the keys was Sylvia "Siren" Sinclair, a synth sorceress whose fingers danced on her Moog synthesizer like a mystic weaving spells. Her ethereal melodies added a celestial layer, elevating the band's sound to astral heights. She was the heartbeat of the cosmos, grounding their psychedelic journeys with pulsating rhythms. Then there was Johnny "Vortex" Vaughn, a six-string shaman who conjured storms with his Fender Stratocaster. His solos were like lightning bolts, electrifying the air with a raw, untamed energy. Anchoring this cosmic congregation was Max "Pulse" Preston, a rhythmic alchemist on drums. His beats were the backbone, pounding with the precision of a heartbeat yet unpredictable as a fever dream.
